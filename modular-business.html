Executive summary

MBIZ proposes a coordinated commercial-financing and market-linkage platform that connects selected manufacturers, producers and suppliers with businesses, entrepreneurs, distributors and final consumers.

MBIZ will act as a facilitator and commercial intermediary. It will identify genuine business demand, coordinate supplier relationships, help eligible businesses access finance or equipment through authorised financial partners, and support the route from production to market.

The model links finance to productive assets, stock, inputs, supply arrangements and identifiable markets, rather than treating a loan as a stand-alone cash disbursement.

MBIZ seeks partners to co-design a compliant pilot and to assess a proposed 1.5% facilitation or service charge. This percentage is a concept for discussion, not an approved interest rate, guaranteed return or confirmed fee.

The business problem and opportunity

Small and growing businesses can face limited access to affordable finance, difficulty obtaining productive equipment and reliable stock, weak bargaining power with manufacturers and wholesalers, fragmented distribution and insufficient access to dependable markets.

Manufacturers and suppliers may struggle to reach new buyers, assess demand and coordinate distribution.

MBIZ aims to bring the finance partner, supplier, participating business and market opportunity into one coordinated process. It does not replace banks, licensed lenders, manufacturers or regulators.

MBIZ’s role: central facilitator and commercial intermediary

MBIZ will coordinate the commercial journey while each regulated or specialist partner keeps its proper role. Depending on the transaction and contract, MBIZ may act as a business introducer, marketplace operator, procurement coordinator, distribution facilitator or disclosed commercial agent. MBIZ will not represent itself as a bank or lender unless separately authorised.

  1. Business need. The applicant describes the business plan, equipment or stock need, budget and intended market. Responsible: the applicant, through the application and business assessment.
  2. Verification. Identity, business activity, demand, affordability, supplier and intended use are checked. Responsible: the authorised finance partner and relevant verifiers.
  3. Supplier match. The approved need is matched with selected manufacturers, producers and suppliers, and quotations are obtained. MBIZ coordinates; the supplier provides specifications, price and delivery terms.
  4. Financing decision. Creditworthiness, security and repayment capacity are assessed, and the request is approved, declined or revised. The licensed bank or lender makes the credit decision and disburses under its own rules.
  5. Procurement. Where approved, the supplier is paid directly or through controlled disbursement and delivery milestones. Financier, buyer and supplier act under a written agreement.
  6. Delivery and market. Delivery is confirmed, distribution is supported and output is connected to buyers. Supplier, business owner and approved distribution partners.
  7. Repayment and reporting. Repayment follows the lender’s contract, and business performance and outcomes are monitored and reported. Borrower and lender; MBIZ provides agreed operational reporting.

Equipment, stock and supplier-facilitated finance

Subject to lender policy and the borrower’s agreement, finance may support machinery, tools, production equipment, agricultural equipment and inputs, storage and processing assets, inventory from approved manufacturers and wholesalers, telecommunications equipment and authorised products, e-commerce stock, packaging, logistics and other eligible productive assets.

Where appropriate, the financier may pay an approved supplier directly or use milestone-based payments. The borrower must receive clear details of the item, price, warranty, delivery, security, repayment obligation and all charges before accepting finance.

Selected manufacturers, producers and suppliers

MBIZ intends to build a vetted supplier network. Onboarding may consider product quality, manufacturing capacity, stock availability, price competitiveness, after-sales service, warranty, delivery capability, regulatory approvals and track record.

Supplier agreements should specify product standards, price, stock confirmation, delivery timelines, returns, defects, warranties, payment terms and dispute resolution. Supplier selection should be based on documented criteria and disclosed commercial relationships.

Market access, products and distribution

MBIZ will help identify market demand and organise the movement of products from manufacturers and suppliers to businesses, distributors, retailers and final consumers. Activities may include demand aggregation, order coordination, product listing, local sales partnerships, distribution planning, logistics coordination, customer support and transaction documentation.

The pilot should focus on products with demonstrable demand, reliable supply, acceptable margins and manageable delivery risk.

Proposed MBIZ digital token and payment framework

MBIZ is exploring a digital token as a common transaction and settlement instrument within its business ecosystem. The intended design is an MBIZ-first payment model for eligible network transactions, subject to legal classification, regulatory approval, payment-system review, consumer safeguards and technical security assessment.

No participant should be required to acquire, hold or use a token until the relevant legal and regulatory permissions have been obtained and the operating arrangements approved. Until then, transactions should use lawful, approved payment channels, and the token concept remains a proposal for regulatory and institutional review.

Before any live token payment or settlement activity, MBIZ will need specialist advice on token classification, issuance and transfer rules, custody, exchange and conversion, AML/CFT, sanctions screening, consumer disclosures, tax, data protection, cybersecurity, smart-contract audits, wallet recovery and complaint handling.

The token must not be described as legal tender, a deposit, a guaranteed store of value or a guaranteed investment return.

Proposed 1.5% facilitation cost and other charges

MBIZ proposes a 1.5% facilitation or service charge as an initial commercial assumption for eligible transactions. This is for modelling and negotiation only. It is not a confirmed tariff, interest rate, lender fee, statutory charge or promise that every transaction can be serviced at that rate.

The final fee must be tested against operating costs, transaction size, fraud and default exposure, payment-provider charges, taxes, affordability and value delivered. Contracts must state who pays, what service is covered, when it is earned, whether it is refundable and how it is disclosed. It must not be charged twice under different labels.

Loan interest, lender origination fees, insurance, late-payment charges, taxes and third-party payment fees are separate. They must be determined and disclosed by the authorised provider. MBIZ will not set a lender’s interest rate.

Governance, risk management and consumer protection

Pilot policies and contracts should cover:

  • Clear responsibilities between MBIZ, the lender, the supplier, the borrower and the payment provider
  • KYC and business verification, and AML/CFT responsibilities
  • Credit assessment and responsible lending
  • Supplier due diligence and conflicts of interest
  • Data privacy and secure recordkeeping
  • Transparent pricing, complaints, refunds, returns and warranties
  • Fraud prevention and cybersecurity
  • Wallet and key controls, if a token is approved
  • Independent audit, reconciliation and performance reporting

MBIZ will not guarantee loan approval, business income, profits, token value or investment returns.

Proposed pilot programme

  1. Design and readiness (weeks 1 to 4). Legal and regulatory review; select sector and location; agree partner roles; draft contracts; establish baseline and workflow.
  2. Onboarding (weeks 5 to 8). Onboard a limited group of verified suppliers and businesses; collect quotations and plans; train participants; test processes.
  3. Controlled transactions (weeks 9 to 16). Run limited transactions through authorised finance and payment partners; monitor procurement, delivery, repayment and customer experience.
  4. Evaluation (weeks 17 to 20). Review costs, outcomes, defaults, delivery, complaints and inclusion; recommend changes before expansion.

Performance indicators

MBIZ will report on:

  • Number of verified businesses and suppliers
  • Number and value of finance applications and approvals
  • Equipment and stock delivered, and on-time delivery rate
  • Verified end-use of financed assets
  • Repayment and arrears
  • Supplier payment time
  • Measurable changes in business sales and output
  • Jobs or livelihoods supported
  • Complaints and resolution time
  • Operating costs and fee income
  • Fraud and security incidents

Targets should be agreed after the pilot scope and risk appetite are selected. No impact figures should be stated before baseline data exists.
